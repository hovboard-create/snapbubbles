Bubble Wrap Appreciation Day

Bubble Wrap Appreciation Day falls on the last Monday of January every year. It's not a federal holiday, you don't get the day off work, and yet — for one day, popping a sheet of plastic in public is socially acceptable. Here's where the day came from and what people actually do to celebrate.

When is it?

Always the last Monday of January. The 2026 date is January 26, 2026. The 2027 date will be January 25, 2027. The day moves with the calendar but always sits on a Monday — partly because the founders wanted to give offices a Monday morning event, and partly because a sheet of bubble wrap is a reasonable Monday-morning antidote.

Where it came from

The day was created in 2001 by Spirit 95 FM, a radio station in Bloomington, Indiana. The story goes: the station received a shipment of microphones packed in bubble wrap. The on-air staff, naturally, started popping it on the air. Listener calls poured in. The station declared the date "Bubble Wrap Appreciation Day" and the idea stuck.

Sealed Air Corporation (the original manufacturer of bubble wrap, see the full origin story here) acknowledged the day shortly after, sponsored related events, and the holiday quietly went international. The exact level of corporate involvement has varied over the years; the day has always felt more grassroots than commercial, which is part of its charm.

What people actually do

There's no canonical celebration ritual. Common observances include:

  • Office popping sessions. Someone brings in a roll of bubble wrap; the whole team pops their way through it during the morning meeting.
  • Schoolroom science lessons. Some teachers use the day to introduce simple physics concepts — air pressure, polymers, and why a pop is a pop.
  • Bubble wrap fashion contests. An ironic tradition started by some workplaces — outfits made from bubble wrap, judged on creativity. Yes, it's silly. That's the point.
  • Sealed Air's annual Bubble Wrap Competition for Young Inventors. A long-running contest where K–8 students design inventions using bubble wrap. Real prizes, real winners.
  • Just popping some bubble wrap. Most common celebration by far. No costumes required.

Celebrating without buying anything

If you don't have a fresh sheet of bubble wrap on hand and don't want to order packaging just to pop it, virtual works fine. Snap Bubbles runs in any browser, has procedurally-generated pop sounds (variable pitch like real bubble wrap), and supports three modes if you want to make a small game of the celebration.

For the office observance specifically, Survival mode tends to land — it's competitive, takes about 60 seconds per round, and shareable scores create a small leaderboard moment that fits a coffee-break window.

Why a holiday for bubble wrap, of all things?

A handful of small holidays exist for a reason: they create permission. Most office norms discourage popping bubble wrap in shared spaces. The holiday creates one designated day where it's acceptable, even encouraged. That's the actual function — not commemoration of the invention, but a small carved-out window for a universally enjoyable pleasure that's normally embarrassing.

Also, the day is unusually inclusive. There's no religious component, no political angle, no commercial obligation. You don't need to buy a card. You don't need to call your mother. You can participate alone, with coworkers, with kids, with strangers at a party. Few holidays clear that bar.

The bottom line

Mark your calendar for the last Monday of January. Bring or open a sheet of bubble wrap (or open a browser tab, if you're at work and trying to be quiet). Pop a few. Notice if your shoulders drop. That's the celebration.
